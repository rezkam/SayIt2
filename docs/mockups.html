Concept A · what you asked for

Drop Pill

A black pill slides out from under the notch. Bars move with your voice, and the newest words scroll through it. It slides back up the moment the text lands.

Hold vs hands-free
Hands-free adds a small square at the end: "tap to stop".
Finishing
Bars turn into a slow wave and the text dims until the final arrives.
Cost
Smallest. One click-through panel, about 80 lines.
Risk
Low. On a display without a notch it hangs from the top edge instead.

Concept B · wild

Caret Tag

The indicator lives at your text cursor. A small tag grows out of the caret and carries the live words exactly where they are about to be typed. Your eyes never leave the sentence.

Hold vs hands-free
Same small square at the end of the tag.
Cost
Highest. It has to ask each app where the caret is, and reposition as you scroll.
Risk
Many apps (browsers, Electron apps, terminals) do not report a caret position. There it must fall back to concept A, so you would see two different indicators.

Concept C · wild

Horizon

No pill at all. A thin line of light along the top edge of the screen spreads out from the notch as you speak, and the words appear as a subtitle at the bottom of the screen.

Hold vs hands-free
Hold: wings that breathe with your voice. Hands-free: the line locks to the full width.
Cost
Medium. Two panels instead of one.
Risk
The line is easy to miss on a bright window, and the words sit far from where you type. Try a colored accent with this one.

Concept D · least on screen

Menu Bar Ticker

Nothing floats. The menu bar icon itself turns black, grows, and scrolls the live words like a ticker. When you stop, it shrinks back to the icon.

Hold vs hands-free
Same small square, inside the menu bar item.
Cost
Low. No panel, only the status item.
Risk
If macOS hides the icon (behind the notch, or the launcher issue seen with the old app), there is no indicator at all. It also pushes neighboring icons sideways while it grows.
